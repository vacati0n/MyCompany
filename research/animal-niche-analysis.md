## 1. Made-for-kids exposure — the priority question, quantified

`niche-recommendation.md` §2.4 flagged this as the largest unquantified economic risk in the nature/science option. It is now quantified. **The headline: the risk is real and expensive, but it is driven by *treatment*, not by *subject matter*. Neither YouTube nor the FTC names animals, wildlife or nature as a child-directedness factor anywhere in their published criteria.**

### 1.1 What actually triggers the designation — the complete first-party factor list

**[OFFICIAL POLICY]** YouTube, *"Determining if your content is 'made for kids'"*, <https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9528076>, read 2026-09-26. The definition:

> "A video is child directed (which we call 'made for kids') if: Children are the primary audience" or "the video is still directed to children based on the factors below."

The factors, verbatim and complete:

> - "Subject matter of the video (e.g. educational content for preschoolers)"
> - "Whether children are your intended audience (e.g. as indicated in your video metadata)"
> - "Whether the video includes child actors or models"
> - "Whether the video includes characters, celebrities, or toys that appeal to children"
> - "Whether the language of the video is intended for children to understand"
> - "Whether the video includes activities that appeal to children, such as play-acting, simple songs or games"
> - "Whether the video includes songs, stories, or poems for children"
> - "Whether the content is advertised to children"
> - "Any other information you may have to help determine your video's audience"

**[OFFICIAL]** The underlying legal test is the FTC's COPPA "directed to children" standard. FTC, *Complying with COPPA: Frequently Asked Questions*, <https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions>, read 2026-09-26. The Commission's factors are: subject matter and visual content; **animated characters or child-oriented activities**; music or audio content; age of models; child celebrities or celebrities who appeal to children; language and other characteristics; advertising directed to children; competent and reliable empirical evidence about audience composition; and evidence regarding the intended audience.

**[OFFICIAL] The single most important sentence for this decision**, from the same FTC page:

> "Your website or online service will not be considered 'directed to children' just because some children visit your site or use your service."

**[OFFICIAL POLICY]** And YouTube's own FAQ, <https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9684541>, read 2026-09-26, states that content is **not** automatically made for kids merely because it is family-friendly, appropriate for all ages, involves activities associated with children, or might be incidentally viewed by children. Content is likely made for kids if it includes *"actors, characters, activities, games, songs, stories… that reflect an intent to target children."*

**[INFERENCE — the finding that matters] Animals, wildlife and nature appear nowhere in either list.** The word "animal" does not occur in YouTube's factor list, in YouTube's made-for-kids FAQ, or in the FTC's COPPA FAQ factor list. What *does* occur, and what animal content commonly carries, is a set of adjacent triggers: **anthropomorphised or cartoon animal characters** ("characters… that appeal to children", "animated characters"), **simple-language narration** ("language intended for children to understand"), **songs and stories** ("songs, stories, or poems for children"), and **toys**. **The subject is clean; the conventional treatment of the subject is not.**

### 1.2 The "mixed audience" trap — the part that is easy to miss

**[OFFICIAL POLICY]** <https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9684541>, read 2026-09-26:

> "Mixed audience content… is considered a type of made for kids content. This is content that targets children as one of its audiences, even if it's not the main or primary audience."

Against:

> General audience content "could appeal to everyone, but isn't intended specifically for children, or content that is intended for a teen or older audience."

**[OFFICIAL]** The FTC's parallel: a "mixed audience" service targets children under 13 as a non-primary audience and *"falls within the definition of a 'Web site or online service directed to children'."*

**[INFERENCE] This is the real hazard for animal content, and it is sharper than the naive one.** The question is not "is this for kids?" — it is "**is one of the audiences you are targeting children?**" A wildlife channel that softens its language, adopts a cute-animal thumbnail idiom, or names an animal protagonist is not making a kids channel; it may nonetheless be making *mixed audience* content, which YouTube treats as made for kids with the full penalty. **The safe posture is not "not primarily for kids". It is "not for kids at all, at any level of the targeting."**

### 1.3 What it costs — features, and the quantified revenue impact

**[OFFICIAL POLICY]** Complete list of features disabled on content set as made for kids. <https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9527654>, read 2026-09-26:

> "Autoplay on home", "Cards or end screens", "Video watermarks", "Channel Memberships", "Comments", "Donate button", "Likes and dislikes on YouTube Music", "Live chat or live chat donations", "Merchandise and ticketing", "Notification bell", "Personalized advertising", "Playback in the Miniplayer", "Super Chat or Super Stickers", "Save to playlist and Save to watch later"

At **channel level** the same list applies **plus** *"Posts"* and *"Ask Studio"*.

**[OFFICIAL POLICY]** YouTube's own revenue warning, same page: *"Not serving personalized ads on kids content may result in a decrease in revenue for some creators who mark their content as made for kids."*

**[THIRD-PARTY — the best-sourced quantification found] AIR Media-Tech, 2026.** 300 channels, 3,595 monetized channel-months, May 2025 – May 2026, read from YouTube Studio. <https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/which-youtube-niche-makes-the-most-money-in-2026-ranked-by-real-rpm-and-cpm>, read 2026-09-26. Verbatim:

> "Content marked 'made for kids' cannot serve personalized ads under COPPA, which collapses the CPM to $1.59 and the monetized rate to 32%, both the lowest in the study, for a $0.33 RPM, roughly a thirtieth of Education's."

The mechanism is decomposed by the same source, and both halves matter:

| Metric | Education & Science | Kids & Teens | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Median RPM | **$10.22** | **$0.33** | **31×** |
| Median Studio CPM | $5.91 | $1.59 | 3.7× |
| Monetized-playback rate | **77%** (best in study) | **32%** (worst in study) | 2.4× |
| Ads per monetized view | 1.84 | not stated | — |
| P25–P75 spread | $2.31 – $19.50 | $0.16 – $0.88 | — |

**[INFERENCE] The penalty is multiplicative, not additive: the ad price falls ~3.7× *and* the share of views that carry any ad falls ~2.4×.** That is why the RPM ratio (31×) is far worse than the CPM ratio (3.7×) — the part creators consistently under-estimate. **[CAUTION]** AIR's "Kids & Teens" is a *content category*, not a set of MFK-designated videos, so it is a proxy; but AIR attributes the mechanism explicitly to the MFK/COPPA personalised-ads ban, and a 32% monetized-playback rate is a direct signature of that ban. It is the best-evidenced figure available and is used as the downside anchor.

**[THIRD-PARTY] Corroborating but weaker estimates**, recorded and not used as anchors: *"$1–3 RPM versus $5–15 for general-audience content"* and reported drops of 50–80%, with some creators citing >95%. <https://gyre.pro/blog/how-to-monetize-a-youtube-kids-channel>, <https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320340/20260713/ai-kids-cartoon-gold-rush-has-hidden-tax-coppa-cuts-revenue-80.htm>. **[INFERENCE]** These bracket AIR's figure from the optimistic side. Plan on the AIR number.

**[INFERENCE] The non-revenue cost is strategically worse than the revenue cost, given `niche-recommendation.md` §3.3.** That analysis found the **binding constraint on this business is reaching 1,000 subscribers, not watch hours**. Made-for-kids removes **the notification bell, end screens and cards, Save to playlist / Watch later, comments, and channel Posts** — i.e. **it removes almost every mechanism by which a viewer converts into a returning subscriber.** A made-for-kids designation therefore does not merely cut RPM by ~31×; **it attacks the exact variable already identified as the bottleneck.** This is a compounding failure, not a parallel one, and it was not visible in the prior analysis.

### 1.4 Can the creator's own designation be overridden? Yes — and the FTC can act against the creator independently

**[OFFICIAL POLICY]** <https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9684541>, read 2026-09-26:

> "We trust you to set your audience accurately, and we'll only override your audience setting choice in cases of error or abuse."

**[OFFICIAL POLICY]** <https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9527654>, read 2026-09-26:

> "We use machine learning to help us identify videos that are clearly directed to young audiences… we may override your audience setting choice in cases of error or abuse."

Once YouTube has overridden the setting, the creator cannot change it back without appealing.

**[OFFICIAL POLICY]** <https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9528076>: *"Do not rely on our systems to set your audience for you because our systems may not identify content that the FTC or other authorities consider to be made for kids."* And: *"Failure to set your content appropriately may result in consequences on YouTube or have legal consequences under COPPA and other laws."*

**[INFERENCE] Read together, the exposure is two-sided, which is the genuinely uncomfortable part.** Setting "not made for kids" on content a regulator would call child-directed is not a safe default — it is the *riskier* error, because it carries statutory exposure and not merely a revenue haircut.

**[THIRD-PARTY] The enforcement is live and it lands on the uploader, not on YouTube.** In September 2025 the FTC obtained a **$10 million** settlement from Disney for failing to label YouTube videos as made for kids, with Disney required to run an "Audience Designation Program" reviewing every video individually. Reporting summarises the allocation of risk as: the FTC *"won't come after YouTube. It'll come after you."* <https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/09/17/disney-ftc-fine-youtube-videos-child-safety/>, read 2026-09-26. The named content was Disney/Pixar film clips, not wildlife.

**[INFERENCE/ESTIMATE] Practical calibration of the override risk.** No first-party or third-party source was found reporting systematic MFK auto-classification of adult-framed wildlife or nature documentary content. The reported false-positive clusters are around cartoon and game IP (e.g. Sonic-related content) and animation, not documentary nature. **The classifier is described as targeting videos "clearly directed to young audiences"** — a high bar, and an adult-register wildlife documentary is not near it. **Confidence: medium-high that an adult-framed treatment is not auto-flagged; low that anything can be guaranteed, because "empirical evidence of the video's audience" is a live factor and the classifier is not published.**

### 1.5 Verdict on question 1 — MANAGEABLE BY TREATMENT, not inherent to the subject

**[INFERENCE] MADE-FOR-KIDS RISK IS MANAGEABLE BY TREATMENT.** The basis:

1. **Neither YouTube nor the FTC names animals, wildlife or nature as a factor.** The subject is not on either list. `niche-recommendation.md` §2.4 was correct about the *genre's centre of gravity* and wrong to imply the *subject* is the trigger.
2. **Every trigger that animal content commonly carries is a production choice, not a property of the subject**: cartoon or anthropomorphic characters, named animal protagonists, simple language, songs, playful music, toy tie-ins, child-oriented thumbnails and metadata. Each is separately removable.
3. **The FTC's own carve-out is directly on point** — content is not child-directed "just because some children visit". Incidental child viewership is explicitly not the test.
4. **But the mixed-audience rule means the bar is "not targeting children at all", not "mostly for adults"** (§1.2), and a designation can be overridden on error (§1.4).

**Binding treatment rules, recommended for the publishing gate** (design controls — **[INFERENCE]**, not policy):

| # | Rule | Which factor it defeats |
|---|---|---|
| K-1 | **No anthropomorphism.** No names for individual animals, no invented personalities, no dialogue, no "meet Rocky the otter". | "characters… that appeal to children"; "animated characters" |
| K-2 | **Scientific register throughout.** Binomial nomenclature on first mention, quantified claims, named study or institution attribution, vocabulary pitched at an adult general-science reader. | "language… intended for children to understand" |
| K-3 | **No cartoon or illustrative styling in motion graphics.** Diagrams, charts, range maps, anatomical cutaways — data-visualisation idiom, not illustration idiom. | "animated characters"; "subject matter" |
| K-4 | **No songs, no nursery-register music, no sound-effect comedy.** Score is documentary-register only. | "songs, stories, or poems for children" |
| K-5 | **Metadata discipline.** No "for kids", "cute", "funny", "baby animals", "learn animals" in titles, descriptions or tags. Metadata is a named factor in its own right. | "intended audience (e.g. as indicated in your video metadata)" |
| K-6 | **No play-acting framing, no games, no quizzes, no "can you guess".** | "activities that appeal to children" |
| K-7 | **At least one adult-signalling element per video** — predation and mortality treated factually, conservation economics, physiology, statistics on screen. | "subject matter"; general-audience positioning |
| K-8 | **Monitor the Studio age-demographic report from month one.** A material under-18 share means the treatment is drifting and must be corrected before "empirical evidence of the video's audience" becomes operative. | "empirical evidence of the video's audience" |
| K-9 | **Set "not made for kids" deliberately and per video**, never at channel level by default, and document the reasoning. The Disney settlement makes per-video review the expected standard of care. | FTC/COPPA statutory exposure |

**[INFERENCE] The residual risk that cannot be designed away** is the under-18 audience share drifting upward on its own, because animals attract young viewers regardless of treatment. K-8 detects it; nothing prevents it. **This is a monitoring obligation, not a gate failure.** Quantified downside if it materialises: §4.

## 2. Format admissibility under the originality regime

D-012 left the format open between "cute and funny animals" and "the animal world". **The policy text settles it, and it settles it against compilation in the sharpest possible terms.**

### 2.1 The governing bullets, verbatim

**[OFFICIAL POLICY]** YouTube channel monetization policies, **reused content** sub-category of the **inauthentic content** policy. <https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392>, read 2026-09-26 for this analysis; the same wording was verified 2026-09-18 in `platform-policy-dossier.md` §1.4.

**Scope:**

> "Reused content refers to channels that repurpose content that's already on YouTube or another online source without adding significant original commentary, substantive modifications, or educational or entertainment value."

**NOT ALLOWED — complete list:**

> - "Clips of moments from your favorite show edited together with little or no narrative"
> - **"Short videos you compiled from other social media websites"**
> - **"Collections of songs from different artists (even if you have their permission)"**
> - "Content uploaded many times by other creators"
> - "Promotion of other people's content (even if you have permission)"
> - "Content downloaded or copied from another online source without any substantive modifications"
> - "Songs modified to change the pitch or speed, but are otherwise identical to the original song"

**ALLOWED — complete list:**

> - "Using clips for a critical review"
> - "A scene from a movie where you've rewritten the dialog and changed the voiceover"
> - "Replays of a sports tournament where you explain the moves a competitor did to succeed"
> - "Reaction videos where you comment on the original video"
> - "Edited footage from other creators where you add a storyline and commentary"
> - "Content that primarily features the uploading creator in the video"
> - "Edited footage with audio and visual effects on top of the video's reused content that demonstrates substantive editing"

**[OFFICIAL POLICY]** And from the **generic or repetitive content** sub-category, NOT ALLOWED:

> - "Similar or repetitive content with low educational value, commentary, narratives, or minimal variation across videos"
> - "Videos where characters are put in the same situation over and over again with the same outcome"
> - "Image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value"
> - "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production"

ALLOWED:

> - "Same intro and outro for your videos, but the bulk of your content is different"
> - "Similar content, like a series following a set of characters across episodes or a channel that does product reviews, but in which each video has a distinct storyline, focus, or concept"

**[OFFICIAL POLICY]** Penalty: violation of the channel monetization policies *"may result in monetization being suspended or permanently disabled"* — **channel-level, not video-level.** <https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392>.

### 2.2 Two findings in this text that the CEO should see directly

**[INFERENCE] Finding A — "cute and funny animal compilation" is named, almost word for word.** *"Short videos you compiled from other social media websites"* is the canonical description of the cute-and-funny animal compilation format. It is not an adjacent risk or a matter of degree; it is on the NOT ALLOWED list by name. D-012 anticipated this correctly. **The format is dead on arrival and no amount of narration bolted onto it rescues it, because the ALLOWED counterexamples all require either a critical/analytical frame over specific identified source works, or the uploading creator appearing in the video — and D-006 forbids the latter.**

**[INFERENCE] Finding B — and this one is new and matters far more.** The phrase **"even if you have their permission"** appears twice, on *"Collections of songs from different artists"* and on *"Promotion of other people's content"*. **Licensing does not cure reused content.** These are two separate regimes: copyright asks *may you use this asset*, the originality policy asks *did you make something*. A channel could hold perfect Storyblocks and Epidemic Sound licences for every frame and every bar, and still be a reused-content violation if the assembly adds no original narrative.

**This is the binding constraint on the animal niche and it should be stated plainly: under D-006 the channel does not own a single frame it shoots. Every visual is someone else's footage, licensed. The *only* thing that makes the output original is the script, the narration, the argument, and the original motion graphics.** The originality burden therefore falls entirely on the writing. A wildlife channel whose videos are "nice stock clips + pleasant voice reading generic facts" is **structurally closer to the prohibited case than the engineering candidate is**, because engineering content's data visualisation is itself an original artefact carrying the argument, whereas wildlife footage is decorative to the argument.

**[INFERENCE]** This is a genuine, previously unstated disadvantage of the animal niche relative to `niche-recommendation.md`'s candidate B, and it is scored in §5.

| # | Format | Source of visuals | Verdict | Reasoning |

Lower volume ceiling. Watch the advertiser-suitability line on animal cruelty and poaching imagery — treatment must stay documentary. **[INFERENCE]** |

### 2.4 Verdict on question 2

**[INFERENCE] Compilation is dead. Documentary and explainer work.** Precisely:

- **F1 and F2 are out.** The compilation reading of D-012 is foreclosed by named first-party prohibition, and licensing does not save it.
- **F3 is out as a channel identity** and should be capped at an occasional pillar, if used at all.
- **F4, F5, F6 are admissible**, and **F5 (animal science and behaviour explainers with original motion graphics) is the format that best survives the originality regime**, because it is the only one where the original artefact carries the argument rather than decorating it.
- **The channel's originality lives entirely in the script and the graphics**, because under D-006 it owns no footage. That is a real, structural disadvantage of a footage-led subject and it must be answered with an editorial standard, not with a production standard.

## 3. Stock footage supply — the practical gate, tested at species level

**This section overturns the most reassuring number in `niche-recommendation.md`.** That analysis measured supply with the query `wildlife` on Adobe Stock, got 2,912,274, and concluded *"Visual supply is not a constraint there at all."* **It is a constraint. The aggregate number was measuring the wrong thing, on the wrong library.**

### 3.1 Method, and what changed since the prior attempt

`niche-recommendation.md` §2.2 recorded that Storyblocks — **the committed library** — returned 403 to both direct fetch and browser render, so Adobe Stock was used as a shape proxy. **That limitation has been overcome for this analysis: the Storyblocks search pages were read successfully in a rendering browser on 2026-09-26, and the counts below are the committed library's own.** After roughly 40 queries the host began returning HTTP 403 rate-limit responses, so the battery is incomplete; what was captured before the limit is reported, and the gaps are named in §3.6.

**[OFFICIAL] All counts below are read from the libraries' own live search result pages on 2026-09-26** (Storyblocks `results-count` element; Adobe Stock `<title>` element). They are volatile and will differ on any other date.

**[OFFICIAL — a methodological trap that must be recorded]** Storyblocks' search is **fuzzy and spelling-corrected, not literal**. Two proofs from the live pages, read 2026-09-26:

- The query `saola` returns a page whose own result line reads **"181 results found for saona"** — it silently substituted *Saona*, a Caribbean island. **The true saola count is zero.**
- The nonsense query `xyzzy monkey` returns **26 results**, so a multi-word query is not an intersection. `proboscis monkey` returns 1,172 against `monkey` 3,467 and `proboscis` 1,412 — a relevance blend, not 1,172 proboscis monkeys.

**[INFERENCE] Consequence: every multi-word clip count in this niche's supply assessment — including several in `niche-recommendation.md` §2.2 — is an upper bound of unknown looseness. Only single, distinctive, unambiguous tokens give a trustworthy read.** The species figures above that are single tokens (pangolin 1, narwhal 4, bowerbird 6, okapi 8, platypus 9, binturong 12, cassowary 12, axolotl 15, tapir 36) are the trustworthy ones, and they are the damning ones.

**[INFERENCE] What this means for a real production.** A 10–14 minute video needs on the order of **15–30 distinct shots**. The committed library supports that for perhaps two dozen charismatic megafauna (elephant, lion, tiger, wolf, whale, penguin, monkey, shark, bear, eagle) and for generic habitat plates. **It cannot support a single video about a pangolin, a narwhal, an okapi, a platypus, a bowerbird or a cassowary.** Those are exactly the species an interesting animal channel wants, because the common ones are already saturated.

- **And the reason is not a stock-library gap. - **Worse, the gap is filled by generative AI.** The Adobe saola page is the proof: a species essentially never filmed returns 84 "clips", every one of them synthetic or a name collision. **[INFERENCE] Any pipeline that sources SEA endemic footage by clip count will silently publish AI-generated animals as documentary evidence.** On a conservation subject that is a credibility failure of the first order, and it is the specific way this pillar would destroy the channel rather than merely under-perform.

### 5.2 Is the moat real at all?

**[INFERENCE] Editorially, yes — and it is the strongest version of the Vietnam vantage anywhere in this programme.** Southeast Asian conservation has genuine, under-covered, English-accessible subject matter: the wildlife trade and its economics, snaring in the Annamites, peatland and mangrove loss, Mekong hydropower and fisheries, illegal timber, protected-area management, species rediscoveries. The operator's regional vantage gives real access to local reporting and local context that a US competitor will not have. This is materially better than the industrial-economics moat, because conservation is a subject the Tier-1 audience *already cares about* and the industrial one is not.

**[INFERENCE] Visually, no.** Under D-006 the operator may not film. The moat therefore has to be carried entirely by script, sourced data and original graphics — and the one visual asset that would make it distinctive (footage of the animals in question) is the asset that does not exist.

**[INFERENCE] The resolution, and it is a real one.** A **conservation and wildlife-economics pillar (format F6)** works *precisely because it does not need the animal on screen.* Its visuals are landscape, habitat loss, ports, markets, rangers, maps, population charts and trade-flow diagrams — all of which are abundantly supplied (`wildlife conservation` returns 371,970 on Adobe Stock; `mangrove` 5,658 and `rainforest canopy` 2,479 on Storyblocks). **A pillar about the *system* around an animal is footage-feasible where a pillar about the *animal* is not.**

**[OFFICIAL POLICY] One treatment caution.** Poaching, trafficking and cruelty imagery runs into advertiser suitability. YouTube's guidelines treat sensitive subjects on **treatment, not topic**: allowed includes *"news reporting, documentary content or discussions about a sensitive event"*; prohibited is content that *"profits from or exploits"* it. <https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278>. **[INFERENCE]** Documentary register keeps this pillar green; shock-led framing does not. This is the same treatment rule already recorded in `niche-recommendation.md` §1.4 and the editorial gate can enforce it.

### 5.3 Verdict on question 5

**[INFERENCE] The SEA angle is admissible as ONE pillar, in the conservation/wildlife-economics form only, and it must be built to avoid needing footage of the endemic species it discusses.** Attempting a species-led SEA pillar — "the saola", "the Javan rhino", "the Irrawaddy dolphin" — fails the D-006 gate outright, and the failure mode is not an empty timeline but an **AI-illustrated fake**. Cap it at 2–3 videos a month, the same reduction `niche-recommendation.md` §8.4 already recommended for the Vietnam pillar on the engineering channel, and for the same reason.
