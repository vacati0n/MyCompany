# Architecture Decision Record D-009

## Metadata

- ADR ID: D-009
- Title: The timeout, hold and clock enumeration, with the model part bound proposed for ruling
- Date: at emission in run-dfcae1756c32
- Status: Proposed
- Owners: architect (producer); omn-tech-lead (accepting owner of the Design Gate)
- Related Work Items: MC-11; the supplied execution plan's design tasks

## Context

- Problem statement: A local model run, long decodes and large hashes must not sit in a transaction or the 30-second command timeout.
- Business and technical constraints: C-003, C-005, C-016.
- Current architecture baseline: F-004, F-034, F-036.

## Decision

- Selected option: O-001
- Decision statement: Every record is one short transaction after its work; the model part bound is proposed at 350 s on the monotonic clock, twice the measured 175 s; hashing is in process with no bound; delivered bounds are borrowed for registration and measurement; every instant is the datastore's.
- Scope of impact: M-004, M-005, M-007.

## Alternatives Considered

1. O-002: Model process started by a sibling type
- Benefits: The model's invocation sits apart from the media tool
- Risks: Duplicates the delivered bound and process-tree termination; widens the one-starter assertion
- Why not selected: Ties on hard constraints and impact; loses on reuse

2. O-004: Recordings read in place from the operator's folder
- Benefits: No copy of the recordings
- Risks: Bytes outside the writer's promote-once control between hash and decode
- Why not selected: Ties on hard constraints and impact; loses on reuse

## Consequences

- Positive outcomes expected: Every wait ends under a named outcome and no record waits on a process.
- Tradeoffs accepted: A part slower than the bound fails the stage.
- Risks introduced: R-005

## Validation Plan

- Metrics to monitor: Open transactions observed during processes, decodes and hashes.
- Verification checkpoints: Stalled stand-in and skewed-clock tests in P-019.
- Rollback or reversal conditions: Any record written by a process clock, or any transaction open while a process runs.
