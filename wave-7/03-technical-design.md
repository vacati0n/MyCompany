### 5.3 Selected Approach

- Selected: O-002, one admission transaction on one reserved datastore instant, a benchmark record whose cost and latency are the operation's own, evidence ranked from one statement, a pure cost controller, a recorded tier downgrade, retirement of caller-less readings and a seventh resource.
- Structural change:
  - Admission transaction (D-001). The boundary opens its transaction BEFORE resolution and keeps it to commit. Its first statement reserves the booking instant: the later of the datastore's clock and the record horizon, taken under a shared hold on the horizon kept to the end of the transaction, exactly as the recorder stamps today (F-005, F-021), and held in a transaction-local datastore setting so that no caller code carries it. The month of that instant is the booking month. On the same transaction, at that instant, M-006 reads the unit prices in force, the controller readings and the evidence; resolution runs on them with the reserved instant as its instant, so a held outcome's escalation instant is the reserved instant plus the request's hold timeout (F-011). The provider is then invoked, the transaction still open; the recorder books the operation at the reserved instant where the transaction holds one and stamps as delivered otherwise (M-005); the decision record, the audit entry and the delivered budget evaluation for the reserved month follow, and the transaction commits. Because the horizon is held shared from reservation to commit, no closure can raise it past the reserved instant (closures never wait), so the booking check passes and the operation books into the month it was admitted against (C-009). The chain head is taken only at the first append, after the call returns, so appender serialisation is unchanged (C-019). Refusals, holds, deferrals and substitutes use the same transaction and commit without a provider call. Where the transaction is lost during a call, the outcome is recorded on a fresh transaction under a fresh reservation, and its decision record states that the admitted month's reservation was lost and re-evaluates against the booked month (R-029). Routes, forbidden sources, availability and accounts stay read through their delivered ports.
  - Benchmark record (D-002). A corpus-entry register holds an entry identifier, its task class and the datastore's registration instant and no content column, so an entry exists only as a reference, as a fixture in demonstration stores (C-001). An observation row carries its entry and task class (a composite reference to the register, so the two agree), the operation that ran it (unique, with its route and model, a composite reference to the operation record so the three agree), its quality in a row-encoded case, and its observed instant, stamped by the datastore under the shared horizon hold, booked into that instant's month, refused below the horizon or before the referenced operation's instant, and write-once. Quality takes exactly one of three shapes: an observed value with a non-zero amount and a unit; an observed zero with a unit and no amount; or unmeasured with a reason from the delivered closed pair and a non-blank detail and no amount or unit; every other shape is refused by a table check (F-020, C-003). Cost and latency are not columns: the benchmark reading derives them from the referenced operation, the cost an observed value or observed zero in its currency where the operation's cost is stated and unmeasured naming the unpriced unit kinds where it is not, and the latency the operation's recorded duration in milliseconds (C-005). The task class set is closed at the six representative tasks the master plan names (F-037); it refines the capability class rather than mapping to it, so a request names its task class and the comparison key is the pair of the candidate routes' capability class and that task class. A store with no observation reads every quantity of every route and task class unmeasured, naming the register, the route and the task class looked for. No path writing or reading the record references the capability assembly (F-033).
  - Evidence selection (D-003). M-015 ranks among the candidates that survive the delivered steps one to four within the best eligible tier class (D-005). For each candidate it takes, from the evidence table read in one statement on the admission transaction, the minimum observed quality and the maximum observed cost of its observations for the request's task class (A-008). The evidence basis applies only where every candidate has at least one observation, none of a candidate's observations is unmeasured in quality or cost, every quality is in the floor's 0 to 100 rating unit and every cost is in the request ceiling's currency; an observed zero counts as observed and is ranked as zero. The selection is then the cheapest candidate whose minimum observed quality meets the floor, ties broken by route tier then identifier; where no candidate meets the floor on observation the request is held with the delivered floor reason, never served below it. Otherwise the selection is the delivered route-tier ordering exactly, labelled configured and naming each unmeasured quantity, each unit mismatch, or the absent task class. Latency is recorded and read but not ranked. The configured rating is never presented as an observed quality. The decision record carries the basis, the task class, every candidate with its position, its configured rating, its observed quality and cost in their cases and its observation count, and the identifiers of the observations ranked on, so the inputs read back identical. No observation minimum is applied and the count is stated (A-002, C-018). The route register is read, never written.
  - Cost controller and unmeasured readings (D-004). The governing readings of an operation are its channel reading and the company reading of the booking month, read in one statement on the admission transaction at the reserved instant. The channel reading carries the channel budget amount recorded for the month, or not recorded; the booked spend of the channel; and, where both are stated, the utilisation the datastore computes. Booked spend reads observed, observed zero where no operation is booked into the month, because the operation record is the complete ledger of booked metered operations (A-005, F-007), and reads unmeasured, naming the operations, where any operation booked into the month for the scope carries an unstated cost. The company reading carries the company's booked spend against the recorded metered allotment of USD 34.42 (F-016) as the interim basis while the standing charge has no recorded home, stated on every output with what it covers, because governing against the full envelope would admit metered spend the standing charge then pushes past the envelope (C-014, Q-002). M-014 maps each reading to one action: a recorded amount below 50 percent none, at 50 the delivered alert only, at 75 downgrade, at 90 defer, at 100 refuse; a missing amount refuses with the amount-not-recorded reason naming the channel and month; unmeasured spend refuses with the spend-unmeasured reason naming the scope and month; the applied action is the most severe demanded by any reading, ordered none, alert only, downgrade, defer, refuse. The thresholds and the mapping are code constants reused from the delivered threshold set (F-015); the controller takes no configuration dependency, which M-025 asserts. Under refuse, defer or an unmeasured reading, resolution is restricted to hold-and-escalate and non-AI substitute targets, so work costing nothing stays admissible (F-014); where none survives, a refusal returns the refused outcome naming threshold or reason and scope, and a deferral returns the held outcome with the deferral reason and the reserved instant plus the hold timeout as its escalation instant, both with a zero-cost operation row and the decision record (A-011). The department budget stays in the delivered headroom used at resolution step four, read in the same statement; a channel with no amount never reaches that step for a metered route, so a missing budget no longer reaches the exceeded reason. A provider route whose model lacks a price in force at the reserved instant for any of the input, output and cached unit kinds is removed at step four with its own reason rather than estimated at zero (F-010). The recorder records whether each operation's cost is stated: false where a consumed unit kind had no price row in force or any other unit was consumed (F-006). No controller action reaches a compliance or copyright control, because its only effects are on route choice inside the boundary and on the outcome it returns (C-013).
  - Tier rule and downgrade (D-005). Among the survivors of steps one to four, resolution forms classes: those stating the requested tier, those stating a higher tier, and untiered routes, ordered in that order; below the downgrade action the best non-empty class is ranked by evidence or route-tier order, and a route stating a lower tier is never served, so where only lower-stated routes survive the request is held with the no-route-at-requested-tier reason (C-007). Under the downgrade action, for a routine request, the preferred class is the routes stating the highest tier strictly below the requested one; where it is empty, or the request is critical, the ordinary classes apply and the decision records that no downgrade was available and why. An untiered route is never chosen as a downgrade. Every resolved outcome carries its tier outcome, at requested tier, higher, untiered, or downgraded, and the decision record holds it.
  - Tier distribution (D-006). M-009 reads, inside the delivered close-then-read transaction, per served tier and for operations stating none, the operation count, the input, output and cached unit sums, the cost sum and the count of unstated costs, with the period totals. M-021 composes each part: every part unmeasured, naming the period, where the period holds no operation; otherwise counts and unit sums observed, observed zero for a part with none, and cost observed where every cost in the part is stated, observed parts summing exactly to the period total. Each reading carries the assumed split stated over the L1 and L2 and the L3 and L4 levels with the recorded per-item token figures, labelled an assumption (F-037), the count of operations carrying a served tier, a statement that the relation of levels to tiers is not recorded so the reading decides no split (A-009), and its finality.
  - Finality (D-007). The item cost, period cost, cost by capability class, deterministic-set cost, the period headroom member, the three-state period cost and the budget reader are retired from the ports under their own coexistence condition, because none has a production caller (F-024, F-025); the operation partition already carries cost by capability class and deterministic-set cost with finality. The period summary and the served-tier list each return their month closure from the same close-then-read transaction, so the report service's period cost, served tiers and tier ratio take finality from the read that produced the figure. No delivered horizon rule changes.
  - Seventh resource and gate record (D-008, D-009). The seventh resource makes the gate position mandatory, which validates every existing row back-filled by the sixth; refuses any update or delete of a gate transition; and replaces the from-state trigger function so that, under the item-row hold it already takes, it also refuses a transition of an item version at an instant already recorded for that version, under its own constraint name. M-010 maps that name to the named conflict type carrying a same-instant reason, and M-026 maps it to its own step refusal; the first transition admitted under the hold is the one recorded. The sixth resource's header is corrected in place to state that it adds and back-fills the order column and that reversing it loses ordering once a later transition exists; no statement of it changes (F-032).
  - Production and publishing keys (D-010). A company scope form names exactly one company register identifier, beside the delivered channel scope form. The configuration store admits a production key or a publishing key only under a company scope and only where its own delivered rule finds no control; a base key keeps its delivered pairing; a channel key keeps its own. No fragment rule changes (C-012).
  - Recorded amounts (D-011). A recorded-amount type in the accounting namespace has a recorded shape, carrying the amount, its currency and the register or constant it was recorded in, and a not-recorded shape carrying what was looked for and no value. The channel budget reading's amount and the company reading's envelope take it, and every controller output presents amounts in it, apart from observed spend (F-017).
  - Version (D-012) and month-end demonstration (D-013). The version is proposed as 1.4.0 for omn-tech-lead's decision (Q-001). The month-end demonstration runs in the persistence test assembly, in the delivered sequential collection, with a stand-in provider declared there, the capability assembly's internals being made visible to that assembly; the stand-in raises the record horizon past the month end during its call, making no metered call.
- Rationale: O-002 is one of four options satisfying every hard constraint; all four tie on impact surface at seven contract-change modules, and O-002 leads O-003 and O-006 on reuse leverage, 35 rows against 34, and ties O-011 on reuse while carrying one contract change fewer. The selection follows from the third and fifth criteria.
- Highest-scoring rejected alternative and why it lost: O-011 ties O-002 on hard constraints, impact surface and reuse; it loses on migration burden, because it changes the statements of a resource already applied, so a store re-installing reruns a different sixth resource while the seventh keeps every applied resource as it was.
- Tradeoffs accepted:
  - One transaction is held across each provider call, so closures and throughput reads during a call fall back to the stored horizon or read unmeasured; recorded as R-001.
  - An operation books at its admission instant, earlier than its completion; recorded as R-002.
  - Booked spend reads observed zero for a month with no operation while the delivered per-channel cost reading reads unmeasured; recorded as R-020.
  - The company basis is the metered allotment until the owner answers, which refuses earlier than the full envelope would; recorded as R-015.
  - Below the downgrade action a routine request whose only survivors state a lower tier is held rather than served; recorded as R-025.
  - Seven port members and one port are retired; recorded as R-006 and R-030.

## Operational Considerations

- Logging and observability updates: every admission writes its decision record beside its operation row and audit entry on one transaction, carrying the readings, the action, the basis, the candidates and the tier outcome (M-006, M-011); a lost reservation is stated on the fresh transaction's decision record (M-011); every benchmark, tier distribution and month reading states its finality and the horizon it was decided against (M-009, M-022); the seventh resource reports, when applied, how many gate transitions it validated (M-001).
- Error handling strategy: a refusal the design decides is a named outcome rather than an exception, by the six added refusal reasons and the same-instant step refusal (M-017, M-026); a datastore check refusal, an observation off-shape, a decision off its operation's instant, a transition at a taken instant, rolls its own transaction back and surfaces as the datastore's refusal, which M-010 maps to the named conflict for the gate case; a provider failure is recorded on the admission transaction exactly as delivered; a lost admission transaction records the outcome on a fresh one rather than dropping an incurred cost (M-011).
- Security considerations: the change carries integrity rather than confidentiality impact and adds no credential, secret, account or external exposure. No configuration key reaches a threshold, the mapping or an admission, because the controller takes no configuration dependency and M-025 asserts it (C-006, M-014). The production and publishing keys are admitted only under a company scope under their own unchanged rules, and no channel or fragment rule is weakened (C-012, M-008, M-023). Observation and decision shapes are refused for every writer by table checks, so a bypassing writer cannot commit a shape selection would rank (C-003, M-001). No controller action reaches a compliance or copyright control (C-013, M-014). The capability assembly's internals become visible to one more test assembly only (M-012).
- Performance considerations: each provider call holds one connection and one shared horizon hold for its duration, bounded by the delivered client timeout (A-006, M-011); the controller readings and the evidence are one statement each, served by the delivered channel and period index and the observation's route and task class reference (C-004, M-006); the same-instant check is one indexed lookup under the item hold the trigger already takes (M-001). No figure is asserted beyond the constraints named.
- Deployment and operability impact: one additive resource is applied by the delivered installer (M-001), before any process starts; while a provider call is in flight month readings read not yet final from the stored horizon and throughput reads unmeasured, honestly (M-009); demonstrations run only against throwaway stores in the separate demonstration database (C-017).

### Test Strategy Focus Areas

- One clock: with the process clock a day behind and a day ahead, every observation, decision, operation and escalation instant equals a datastore instant, and the decision instant equals its operation's booked instant (P-013).
- Month end: an operation admitted before a month end while the stand-in raises the horizon past it during its call is booked into the admitted month, its decision and alerts carry that month, and the stand-in makes no metered call (P-013).
- Shapes: each permitted observation and decision shape round trips; each forbidden shape written directly is refused; an observed zero and an unmeasured quantity render apart side by side (P-001, P-013).

### Rollout and Rollback

- Rollout: the seventh resource is appended and applied before any process starts; no row is written by it; no observation, entry, decision, budget amount or configuration value is recorded in the company's own store by this change.
- Rollback: reverting the code restores the delivered boundary, readings and ports; the seventh resource's objects are dropped in reverse order while empty, and restore-to-point is the reversal once a row exists; the mandatory position and the write-once gate check can each be dropped without loss.
