# Architecture Decision Record D-005

## Metadata

- ADR ID: D-005
- Title: Resolution serves no route stating a lower reasoning tier than requested except as a recorded controller downgrade for a routine request
- Date: 2026-10-09
- Status: Proposed
- Owners: omn-tech-lead as the accepting owner of the Design Gate under the producer exclusion rule; architect as the producing agent
- Related Work Items: MC-8; technical design package DESIGN-2026-0008; planner tasks T-004, T-015 and T-029; sequencing constraints P-005 and P-011

## Context

- Problem statement: resolution reads neither the requested tier nor a route's stated tier, so a lower stated tier can already be served unrecorded and a downgrade at 75 percent would be indistinguishable from ordinary resolution.
- Business and technical constraints: C-007 allows a lower stated tier only as a recorded downgrade for a routine request at or above the floor and never onto an untiered route; C-016 requires a delivered behaviour change to be named.
- Current architecture baseline: F-009 records the delivered steps and ordering; F-012 records the requested tier and criticality on the request; F-013 records the tier orders and the stated-tier column; F-038 records the served tier written from the admitting route.

## Decision

- Selected option: O-002.
- Decision statement: among the survivors of steps one to four, routes stating the requested tier, then a higher tier, then none form ordered classes, and the best non-empty class is ranked; a route stating a lower tier is never served without the downgrade action, so where only such routes survive the request is held with the no-route-at-requested-tier reason; under the downgrade action a routine request prefers the routes stating the highest tier strictly below the requested one, and where none survives, or the request is critical, the ordinary classes apply and the decision records that no downgrade was available and why; every resolved route carries its tier outcome.
- Scope of impact: M-013 applies the classes; M-017 declares the tier outcome and the reason; M-028 re-points the delivered resolution checks.

## Alternatives Considered

1. O-007, delivered resolution below the downgrade action and the lowest stated tier at it.
- Benefits: no delivered resolution check changes below 75 percent.
- Risks: a lower stated tier is still served without record.
- Why not selected: eliminated by C-007.

2. O-001, O-003, O-004, O-005, O-006, O-008, O-009, O-010 and O-011, the other options of the package evaluation.
- Benefits: each carries this decision unchanged.
- Risks: each differs from O-002 in another design area only.
- Why not selected: none is decided on this record.

## Consequences

- Positive outcomes expected: a downgrade is a distinguishable, recorded event; a critical request never downgrades; no untiered route is chosen as one.
- Tradeoffs accepted: a routine request whose only survivors state a lower tier is held below the downgrade action.
- Risks introduced: R-005, re-pointed checks; R-025, the new hold.

## Validation Plan

- Metrics to monitor:
  - The count of served routes stating a lower tier than requested without a recorded downgrade; the expected value is zero.
  - The count of downgrades for critical requests or onto untiered routes; the expected value is zero.
  - The count of downgrades below the floor; the expected value is zero.
- Verification checkpoints:
  - Fixture routes stating each tier and none, with routine and critical requests at 75 percent and below it.
  - The list of re-pointed delivered resolution checks with the reason for each.
- Rollback or reversal conditions:
  - Reversed if the owner rejects the new hold; the scope ruling on downgrades is then revisited before any lower tier is served.
