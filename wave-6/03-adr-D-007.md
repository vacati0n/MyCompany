# Architecture Decision Record D-007

## Metadata

- ADR ID: D-007
- Title: Appenders take an exclusive chain-head hold before stamping and reading the head, and a datastore check binds every writer to the current head
- Date: 2026-10-08
- Status: Proposed
- Owners: omn-tech-lead as the accepting owner of the Design Gate under the producer exclusion rule; architect as the producing agent
- Related Work Items: MC-7; technical design package DESIGN-2026-0007; planner tasks T-009, T-021 and T-035; sequencing constraints P-001 and P-009

## Context

- Problem statement: two appenders in flight at once can read the same head hash and both append after it, so chain verification over genuinely concurrent entries reports a break between genuine entries; the fork has been latent since before the previous change.
- Business and technical constraints: C-013 requires a linear chain for every writer, an appender losing a race to append once or report its refusal, and no hold order able to form a cycle against the record horizon; C-015 requires additive change; C-011 forbids re-creating the record horizon.
- Current architecture baseline: F-014 records the head read with no hold after stamping; F-015 records the unique sequence and entry hash and the non-unique predecessor hash; F-013 records the shared horizon hold and the non-waiting exclusive closure; F-038 records the row-hold behaviour; F-010 records that an audited transaction holds no external call.

## Decision

- Selected option: O-002.
- Decision statement: a one-row chain-head record is created in the sixth resource. The appender takes it exclusively before it stamps and before it reads the head, and the hold lasts to the end of its transaction, so appenders serialise and each reads a head no other in-flight appender can extend. A check on the append-only record, named to fire after the delivered horizon check, takes the same hold and refuses an entry whose predecessor hash differs from the entry hash of the highest-sequence entry, or from the genesis value where none exists, so a writer bypassing the appender is refused rather than forking the chain. A waiting appender appends exactly once after the holder ends; a refused bypassing writer reports its refusal with its rolled-back transaction. Hold order: the chain-head hold is the only waited-for exclusive hold, and every exclusive acquisition of the record horizon is non-waiting, so no cycle can form. Historical pairs are neither repaired nor rejected.
- Scope of impact: M-001 adds the record and the check; M-002 takes the hold; M-003 and M-007 keep every exclusive horizon acquisition non-waiting.

## Alternatives Considered

1. O-004, a hold the appender alone takes, with no datastore check.
- Benefits: no schema object and the smallest change.
- Risks: a writer bypassing the appender can still fork the chain.
- Why not selected: it violates C-013 and is eliminated.

2. A datastore uniqueness rule on the predecessor hash, considered within O-002.
- Benefits: a declarative statement that no two entries share a predecessor.
- Risks: it cannot be added over a store already holding a forked pair, which the append-only record cannot repair, and it refuses the losing appender's whole business transaction instead of serialising it.
- Why not selected: the hold serialises without refusals, and the head check binds bypassing writers without failing on history.

3. Taking the record horizon exclusively in the appender, considered within O-002.
- Benefits: one row for both purposes.
- Risks: transactions already holding the horizon shared would upgrade and deadlock, and the throughput closure's semantics change.
- Why not selected: a separate row keeps the shared horizon hold intact.

4. O-001, O-003, O-005, O-006, O-007, O-008 and O-009, the other options of the package evaluation.
- Benefits: each carries this decision unchanged.
- Risks: each differs from O-002 in another design area only.
- Why not selected: none is decided on this record.

## Consequences

- Positive outcomes expected: zero pairs of entries share a predecessor after the change, every appender's entry appears exactly once, and verification over concurrent writes reports no break.
- Tradeoffs accepted: audited transactions serialise from their first append to their end; a bypassing concurrent writer is refused rather than serialised.
- Risks introduced: R-006, a long audited transaction stalling writes; R-007, a waiting exclusive horizon acquisition forming a cycle; R-014, checks disabled at session level; R-030, a historical fork; R-033, check firing order.

## Validation Plan

- Metrics to monitor:
  - The count of entry pairs sharing a predecessor among entries written after the change; the expected value is zero.
  - The count of entries per appender in a concurrency run; the expected value is exactly one each, or a reported refusal.
  - The count of deadlocks reported by the datastore during concurrent appends with throughput and month closures; the expected value is zero.
- Verification checkpoints:
  - At least two appenders in flight concurrently against a throwaway store, together with throughput and month reads, followed by chain verification.
  - A direct insert after a superseded head refused by the check, with the delivered horizon refusal still reported by its own reason for an entry below the horizon.
  - Review that every exclusive acquisition of the record horizon is non-waiting.
- Rollback or reversal conditions:
  - Reversed if the hold measurably stalls audited writes; the check and the record are dropped and the delivered appender restored, which reopens the fork the change closed.
