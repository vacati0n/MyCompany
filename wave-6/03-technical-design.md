### 5.3 Selected Approach

- Selected: O-002, configuration-held attributes, a company-level payee record, partitions over carried relations, datastore-booked operations under the record horizon, a datastore-bound chain, a recorded-state gate path and a deterministic copyright-check caller.
- Structural change:
  - Per-channel attributes (D-001). Audience, brand, voice, tone, visual identity, content strategy, schedule and the channel's own risk notes are values in the delivered temporal configuration register (F-003), under a closed channel key set declared once in M-009 beside the delivered key sets and scoped to one channel by a scope naming the channel's register identifier. M-006 admits a channel key only under a channel scope and refuses any other key-scope pairing, so the register's history, validity and changed-by fields carry every change. Language is read from the channel register row, which already holds it (F-002); library registration is read from the library registration register, which already holds it per channel and library and which the rights precondition consumes (F-005, A-003), so neither is a configuration value and the registration precondition stays unsatisfiable from configuration. M-019 composes the profile for a channel present in the register; each configured attribute reads either recorded, with its value, version and validity start, or not recorded, naming the key and the scope looked for, and never a default. The channel key set carries a forbidden-fragment rule in the delivered style (F-037) that refuses any key naming payee, payment, account, registration, condition, approval, gate, refusal, block, publish, dispatch, upload, route, routing, trigger, due, live or enable, so no per-channel key can reach a control (C-008). No component other than M-019 reads a channel key, which M-023 asserts; the schedule and content-strategy values are therefore never read as a due time, a trigger or a routing target, and a channel brought into being by register and configuration rows is neither live nor routed to, because nothing selects work by channel and the entry point takes its channel only from its caller (F-002, C-001). No value for channel one is recorded by this change (C-003).
  - Company-level payee (D-002). A company-level payment-account observation record holds the same three-valued state, mandatory evidence and observation date as the per-channel condition table, keyed by the company and the date (F-006). M-005 builds the delivered condition register (M-028) from the channel's observations of library registration and two-step verification and from the company's observations of the payment account, reached through the channel's mandatory company reference (F-002); every channel of a company therefore resolves the payment-account condition from the same rows, and the register type is unchanged. The per-channel condition table gains a check, validated for new rows only, refusing a new payment-account row; every row already recorded is retained, unread for resolution, so no observation is lost and resolution can only become more refusing than before (A-004). Each channel's risk profile carries a required authored statement, composed by M-019 and not configurable, that the channel shares the company's single payee and payment account with every other channel of the company, naming how many channels the register holds for it; it cannot be configured away because no key reaches it and no record shape holds a per-channel payee (C-007). The other two conditions stay per channel and unchanged.
  - Channel partition of every published reading (D-003). Every reading the company publishes is listed with the record it reads and the relation by which that record reaches a channel: the period cost, the cost by capability class, the deterministic-set cost, the variance against the envelope, the served-tier records and the tier ratio read the operation row's mandatory channel identifier (F-002, F-012); the item cost, the item dossier counts, the stage outcomes, the supply audit, the determinations and each approval's effort read the item's mandatory channel reference (F-002, F-025, F-034); the throughput quantities read the work unit named by the declared lifecycle subject format and the unit's mandatory channel reference (F-016); the revenue-derived figures read a register that carries no channel and stay dark for every channel and for the company, stating so. M-008 declares a channel partition reader returning, for a period, one boundary summary per channel identifier present in the register or in the rows read, each carrying the datastore's aggregation, and M-007 computes every per-channel aggregation and the company aggregation in one datastore statement over the same rows, so the company figure is the exact sum of the channel figures (C-009). A channel identifier present in the rows and absent from the register is reported as its own partition, named as not in the register, and a lifecycle entry whose subject does not name a work unit is reported as unattributed, so no row is dropped from the sum. M-012 decides each per-channel case at one site: a channel absent from the register reads unmeasured naming the register; a month ending at or before the record's earliest entry reads unmeasured naming the record's start (A-006); otherwise a count reads observed, observed zero included, and a cost figure follows the delivered rule, unmeasured where the channel recorded no operation (F-011, A-010). The delivered company-only members are kept unchanged beside the partitioned readings, and every added type sits in the analytics namespaces under the membership assertion (C-004).
  - Budget utilisation (D-004). Each channel's utilisation for a period reads unmeasured naming the missing budget where the budget register holds no channel budget for that period, unmeasured naming the absent operations where the cost reading is unmeasured, and otherwise the datastore's utilisation of the delivered function (F-007). The company reading divides the company cost by the delivered envelope total (F-008) in the datastore. Each of the four thresholds is a count quantity derived at the same site from the utilisation it belongs to — one where reached, zero where not, unmeasured where the utilisation is — using the delivered threshold detection; the delivered idempotent alert per budget, period and threshold stays the only alert written, and no company alert is persisted. The company reading carries a required coverage statement that it covers the metered operations the operation record carries and nothing else, and a standing-commitment quantity that reads unmeasured because no record of a standing commitment exists (F-008), so the standing charge is neither folded in nor presented as zero (C-010, A-003).
  - Approval listing and workload (D-005). M-008 declares an approval queue reader with read members only: the item versions of one channel whose latest recorded gate state is awaiting owner approval, read from the gate-transition record and the item's channel (F-035), and the recorded approvals of one channel and period by decision instant (A-005). The listing exposes no member that writes, presents, decides or transitions, and reads no configuration (C-006). Approval counts and change-request counts are count quantities for a channel known to the register; each approval's three effort components come from the delivered derivation (F-034) at the delivered composing site; a channel with no recorded approval in the period reads one entry whose three components are unmeasured, never zero minutes. No aggregate of minutes, threshold, target or pass line is formed, and the reading carries a required statement that it is as recorded at the read and claims no permanence (C-010).
  - Finality over the operation record (D-006). M-003 stamps each operation's instant from the datastore as the later of its clock and the record horizon, under the same shared hold on the horizon the appender takes (F-013), resolves the price rows at that instant, and books the operation into that instant's month; the recorder returns the stored instant in the delivered instant member (M-040). A check on the operation record in the sixth resource takes the same shared hold and refuses an operation stamped below the horizon or booked into any month other than its instant's, so the rule binds every writer (C-012). A month reading over the operation record attempts the delivered non-waiting closure the throughput reader uses, raising the horizon when granted and otherwise reading the horizon as stored, and reads final only when the horizon is at or after the first instant of the next month; otherwise it reads not yet final, stating that operations may still be booked into it. The rule governs the period cost and every reading derived from it, the served-tier records, the tier ratio, their channel partitions and both budget utilisation readings. M-004 evaluates budgets for the month of the instant the recorder returned, so an alert is raised against the month the operation was booked in. The delivered throughput reading is unchanged.
  - Audit chain (D-007). A one-row chain-head record in the sixth resource is held exclusively by the appender before it stamps and before it reads the head, and the hold lasts to the end of its transaction (F-038), so appenders serialise and each reads a head no other in-flight appender can extend. A check on the append-only record, firing after the delivered horizon check (A-013), takes the same hold and refuses an entry whose predecessor hash differs from the entry hash of the highest-sequence entry, or from the genesis value where none exists, so a writer bypassing the appender is refused rather than forking the chain (C-013). An appender that waits appends exactly once after the holder ends; a bypassing writer that loses is refused with its transaction, which is its reported refusal. Hold order: the chain-head hold is the only waited-for exclusive hold; every exclusive acquisition of the record horizon is non-waiting; so a transaction holding the horizon shared that waits for the chain head waits on a holder that needs only shared holds, which no queued exclusive request can block, and no cycle can form. Historical pairs sharing a predecessor are neither repaired nor rejected, because the check reads the current head only (A-004).
  - Gate path (D-008). M-016 gains a submission member that brings an item version from its recorded state to awaiting rights check where the table admits that edge, which it admits from Draft only (F-018), under a role holding approval presentation (F-022). The presentation for owner approval reads the recorded state and refuses by name, writing nothing but a refusal entry, unless the recorded state is awaiting rights check and the delivered rights precondition is releasable for the item's recorded channel (F-021); a presentation from Draft is therefore refused by the service before the writer is reached, and the writer's own table check remains behind it (F-019). A check on the gate-transition record in the sixth resource holds the item row exclusively for the writing transaction and refuses a transition whose from-state differs from the item version's latest recorded state, Draft where none is recorded, so no caller can claim a state the record does not hold (C-014). The transition table, the publish predicate and the three conditions are unchanged, so a dispatch the item then reaches is still refused by each undischarged condition named individually (C-002).
  - Recorder's first production caller (D-009). M-018 implements the delivered stage handler contract for the copyright-check stage in the rule-determined assembly; it reads the item's recorded assets and its recorded channel's registrations and returns stage evidence of the delivered rights precondition's verdict, performing no metered operation because the rule is already named zero-cost work (F-024). M-017 carries a new item version: it opens the dossier through the delivered recorder where none is opened, submits the item through M-016, runs M-018, records its evidence through the recorder (M-029), and then presents the item through M-016 where the verdict is releasable or sends it back where it is not. The item's channel is read from the recorded dossier and never accepted from a caller (F-025). Each step reads recorded state first, so a step interrupted between two writes resumes from what was recorded. Nothing here touches the publishing sequence, so no position is added after composition (F-032).
  - Clocks, the unclaimed rest and the version (D-010, D-012, D-011). M-015 states on the deferred cycle-time declaration that every stage row recorded so far carries its entry instant from the datastore's clock and its exit instant from the process clock (F-026), so a cycle time must not be read from them without that limit; no duration is computed (F-027) and rows already recorded keep their instants. M-026 adds a check driving the entry point over a unit that is not ready, reaching the unclaimed rest (F-028). M-022 declares one version, proposed here as 1.3.0, for omn-tech-lead's decision.
- Rationale: O-002 is one of five options that satisfy every hard constraint, and it ties O-003 on impact surface at two contract-change modules while leading O-005, O-006 and O-007, which each change one further contract. It leads O-003 on reuse leverage, because it reuses the delivered record horizon and the stamping discipline for the operation record where O-003 reuses neither. The selection follows from the evaluation table on the second and third criteria.
- Highest-scoring rejected alternative and why it lost: O-003 satisfies every hard constraint and ties O-002 on impact surface; it loses on reuse leverage, because it states a limit on every month reading instead of reusing the record horizon, and in consequence no month reading over the operation record, budget utilisation included, is ever final.
- Tradeoffs accepted:
  - Operations are booked at the datastore's instant, so the booking month differs from the occurrence month for an operation committing after the horizon passed that month's end, and delivered checks that record at fixed instants must read the period back; recorded as R-005 and R-009.
  - Appenders serialise from their first append to the end of their transaction; recorded as R-006 and R-007.
  - The company utilisation covers metered operations only and states so, with the standing charge an unmeasured quantity until a record of standing commitments exists; recorded as R-024.
  - The per-channel payment-account rows already recorded are retained and unread, so the payment condition can only become more refusing after the change; recorded as R-003.
  - Throughput partitions by parsing the declared subject format rather than a typed column; recorded as R-012.
  - Stage rows stay on two clocks, with the limit stated rather than removed; recorded as R-026.
  - The rights-check step spans several transactions and resumes from recorded state rather than committing atomically; recorded as R-018.

## API and Data Model Impact

- API changes:
  - M-016, the publication gate service: a submission member is added; the presentation member keeps its name and inputs and gains a closed refusal result naming the recorded state and the reason, in place of an exception for the two refusals it now decides itself; the verdict and publish-evaluation members are unchanged.
  - M-008, the read ports: a channel partition reader and an approval queue reader are added as new interfaces with read members only; no delivered interface gains or loses a member, so no delivered implementer or test double changes for them.
  - M-009 and M-010: a closed channel key set and a channel profile read model are added; the delivered key sets are unchanged.
  - M-011: partitioned read models, a finality member on each month reading, budget and ceiling readings, and approval listing and workload readings are added; the delivered read models are unchanged.
  - M-017, M-018 and M-019: three new deterministic types; M-018 implements the delivered stage handler contract without changing it.
- Contract compatibility notes:
  - M-001 transition strategy. Current shape: five ordered additive resources. Target shape: a sixth additive resource appended after them, applied only after every object it depends on. Compatibility approach: every object is created only where absent, no delivered table, column, view or check is altered or dropped, and every check it adds validates new rows only. Coexistence period: none; the installer applies all six in order. Retirement condition: none, the five stay. Rollback position: the resource's objects are dropped in reverse order, which is lossless while the company-level record is empty and lossy once an observation is recorded there, when restore-to-point is the reversal.
  - M-016 transition strategy. Current shape: the presentation requests the recorded state to awaiting owner approval and relies on the writer to refuse an edge the table lacks. Target shape: it refuses by name unless the recorded state is awaiting rights check and the rights precondition is releasable, and a submission member writes awaiting rights check. Compatibility approach: the only delivered callers are checks (F-020); the check asserting a presentation from Draft keeps its outcome with a named refusal in place of the exception, and fixtures that wrote awaiting rights check directly move to the submission member under M-025. Coexistence period: none. Retirement condition: the direct fixture writes are removed in the same change. Rollback position: reverting the service restores the delivered presentation, and the datastore from-state check, which binds independently, keeps refusing a claimed state the record does not hold.
- Schema or migration changes:
  - The sixth additive resource creates a company-level payment-account observation table, keyed by company and observation date, with the delivered three-valued state check and the mandatory-evidence check, created empty; direction forward; reversible by dropping it while it is empty. Readers during transition: before the resource exists the gate ledger finds no company observation, which resolves the payment account absent and refusing, so the gate is at least as refusing before the change as after it.
  - It adds a check on the per-channel condition table refusing a new payment-account row, validated for new rows only, so every recorded row keeps its meaning; direction forward; reversible by dropping the check. Writers during transition: a fixture writing a per-channel payment-account row is refused and moves to the company-level table.
  - It creates the one-row chain-head record, initialised once, and a check on the append-only record named so it fires after the delivered horizon check; direction forward; reversible by dropping both, after which the delivered appender behaviour returns. Writers during transition: the delivered appender and the changed appender both satisfy the check whenever they are alone; only concurrent writers without the hold are refused.
  - It adds a check on the operation record refusing an operation stamped below the horizon or booked into another month than its instant's; direction forward; reversible by dropping it. Existing operation rows keep their period. Writers during transition: the changed recorder always satisfies it; a writer supplying a stale instant is refused, which is the fixture change M-025 carries.
  - It adds a check on the gate-transition record refusing a from-state that differs from the latest recorded state; direction forward; reversible by dropping it. Existing transitions are not re-validated.
  - No data migration over existing rows arises: no row is copied, moved, rewritten or deleted.

## Operational Considerations

- Logging and observability updates: every added write is accompanied by an append-only entry on its own transaction — the submission, the presentation, the refused presentation, the rights-check stage evidence through M-029 and the send-back (M-016, M-017); the partitioned readings report a channel not in the register and an unattributed lifecycle entry as named partitions rather than dropping them (M-007, M-012); the month readings state not yet final with the horizon they were decided against (M-012); the sixth resource reports, when applied, how many per-channel payment-account rows it retained unread (M-001).
- Error handling strategy: a refusal the design decides is a named result rather than an exception — the presentation's two refusals, a channel absent from the register, an unmeasured utilisation naming the missing budget, and a month not yet final (M-016, M-012); a datastore check refusal — a stale from-state, an entry after a superseded head, an operation below the horizon — rolls its own transaction back and surfaces to the caller as the datastore's refusal, which M-016 maps to its named refusal for the from-state case; a month reading whose closure meets in-flight transactions on every bounded attempt falls back to the stored horizon rather than waiting (C-012); the rights-check step re-reads recorded state at each step so a retry resumes rather than duplicating a write-once row (M-017).
- Security considerations: the change carries integrity rather than confidentiality impact, and no credential, secret, account or external exposure is added. The approval queue port has read members only and reads no configuration, so no route around owner approval exists through it (C-006, M-008). The channel key set's forbidden-fragment rule and the assertion that only M-019 reads channel keys keep every per-channel value away from controls, preconditions and the payee (C-008, M-009, M-023). No record shape admits a new per-channel payment-account value (C-007, M-001). The chain check and the from-state check bind every writer of their record in the datastore rather than by convention (C-013, C-014). The rights check reads the item's recorded channel, never a supplied one (M-017); the delivered publish evaluation still takes its channel from its caller, raised as Q-005. Configuration values are free text interpreted by nothing (A-012).
- Performance considerations: per-channel cost aggregation uses the delivered index by channel and period (C-009); the throughput partition joins each counted entry to its unit by primary key; the chain-head hold serialises audited transactions from their first append to their end, which is bounded by the absence of any external call inside an audited transaction (F-010, A-007, M-002); the from-state check holds one item row per gate write (M-001); month closure is the delivered non-waiting attempt with a bounded retry (M-007). No figure is asserted for any of these beyond the constraints named.
- Deployment and operability impact: one additive resource is applied by the delivered installer (M-001); before it is applied to the company's own store, the store is confirmed to hold no per-channel payment-account observation and no audit pair sharing a predecessor (Q-002); demonstrations run only against throwaway stores in the separate demonstration database (C-016).

### Rollout and Rollback

- Rollout: the change is applied to the company's own store only after Q-002 is answered; the installer appends the sixth resource; no row is written by it except the chain-head record; channel one's values, any budget and any condition are recorded by nothing in this change.
- Rollback: reverting the code restores the delivered services; the sixth resource's checks are dropped in reverse order of creation and its tables dropped while empty; once a company-level observation exists, restore-to-point is the reversal; the from-state, chain and booking checks can each be dropped independently without data loss.
